# Comprehension and low-effort review: 100 synthetic profiles

**Expert projection: about 64/100** (64.3 by the documented arithmetic). This is one independent reviewer applying rules to 100 supplied scenarios. It is not 100 customer sessions or 100 independent pieces of feedback.

Baseline: v25 runtime 7a7af403bd62ea6d10bf056b68a67c5e5cbfe9de; documentation checkout fe55436. The final manifest has 88 readable inputs, six scans and six no-CV inputs, with 27 mobile scenarios. These proportions are assumptions, not customer traffic.

## What the score means

I inspected the current public entry, free checker, private Job Loop, help, walkthrough and pricing source. The reference task is an existing readable CV plus one vacancy, followed by another application and optional private setup. Segment bases are adjusted for the supplied device, time, confidence, familiarity, input, discovery and privacy fields. The JSON records every base, modifier, source line, eight-dimensional score and weighted calculation.

The supplied weights total100: value clarity10, getting started10, first application20, effort saved20, second application15, trust/control10, interface/help10 and operational reliability5. The same weights apply to all profiles. The rubric is 0–19 blocked,20–39 major workaround,40–59 substantial friction,60–74 moderate gaps,75–89 limited friction and90–100 exceptional and repeatedly evidenced.

**Hosted operational reliability is unknown.** Its required numeric score is held at70 as a provisional code-confidence proxy, not an observed service metric. Excluding that5% component and renormalizing gives 64/100. No conversion, NPS, retention, interview, time-saving or willingness-to-pay rate was inferred.

## Highest-value changes

1. **Carry an approved free check into Job Loop.** Returning users and busy professionals face separate source/setup work after receiving first-check value. Offer one explicit Save this approved CV to Job Loop action with reviewable imported source, role and vacancy; retain separate storage/processing consent. (source-backed journey gap; E05, E07.)
2. **Reduce first-use stages and repeated editing decisions.** A dedicated role stage, full-text review and final preview/approval compete with ten-minute use. Collect CV and vacancy together, infer an editable role title, and present one clear review surface followed by document approval. (source-backed task count; conversion impact unverified; E03, E04.)
3. **Use the approved final CV as the easy reuse default.** Source-vs-final choice plus source-update/line-removal options require version management from beginners. Default next-vacancy reuse to the approved final wording, show a clear version label, and keep source restoration in an explicit advanced action. (expert usability proposal; current reuse already exists; E05, E12.)
4. **Hide implementation vocabulary behind one next action.** Claims, runs, packets and provider routes expand the mental model before a candidate sees the next useful document. Show Your experience, Applications and Prepare my CV by default; keep source detail and provider choice expandable with consent at the relevant moment. (source-backed terminology burden; E06.)
5. **Make discovery scope and the free pilot promise match.** Someone expecting a broad job-search assistant can mistake a configured-feed pilot for a complete market search. State the actual connected sources and roles covered before setup; replace complete Job Loop with a precise pilot description and show a clear add-vacancy fallback. (source-backed scope and copy mismatch; E02, E11.)
6. **Integrate scan and no-CV recovery where it is needed.** The twelve supplied scan/no-CV profiles need external conversion or substantial typing before the core check helps. Offer an optional OCR import with explicit processing consent, and a short guided facts-to-first-draft path; measure these small cohorts separately. (source-backed unsupported-input workarounds; E10.)
7. **Give help for the current screen instead of a second application.** Low-confidence users must choose among articles, video, interactive tour, chapters and transcripts. Make one 20–40 second current-screen explanation or concise inline example the default; retain the searchable library and accessible transcript as secondary routes. (expert interpretation of current help hierarchy; E08, E13.)
8. **Remove audience choice from the primary start action.** Student and MBA categories overlap; an example selection also changes entry guidance even though the first task is shared. Use a neutral start CTA and one Students & graduates entry; introduce education/experience-specific examples only where they help the actual CV. (source-backed entry ambiguity; E01, E02.)

The remaining phone work should be validated with actual keyboard, long-text and PDF tasks. A layout breakpoint alone cannot establish low effort. Walkthroughs have useful captions/transcripts, but current-screen accuracy and small-screen playback were not rechecked here.

## Interpretation limits

The public copy still separates students and MBA students, and the example selector changes entry guidance. This is a clarity concern, not proof of conversion loss. The pricing phrase complete Job Loop also sits beside explicit scope limitations elsewhere. Simplifying those promises would help align expectations without claiming integrations that are unavailable.

The main remaining weakness is effort, especially for busy switchers and discovery-led searches. The current product offers useful evidence inspection and safer reusable facts; it still asks the candidate to gather vacancies, manage text versions, confirm facts and review documents. That is useful assistance, but the inspected journey does not substantiate a99%-automated claim.

No runtime files, accounts or candidate data were changed. No paid call or live application action occurred. No other reviewer score or requested target grade was consulted. The proposed score should be replaced or calibrated using real observed tasks before it is treated as a product benchmark.
