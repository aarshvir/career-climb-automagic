# Localized payment and provider accounting correction

The first actual Dodo Test INR payment exposed an integration mismatch before Live localization was released. The customer payment was INR 279,900 minor units, while the corresponding subscription API reported USD 2,947 as its accounting amount. The payment separately reported USD 2,834 settlement and USD 432 settlement tax. These are three different monetary contexts. The previous assumption that `subscription.currency` always equalled the customer charge currency caused webhook rejection.

The correction keeps the original immutable local quote and the succeeded payment's customer currency, exact gross amount and service period as paid-access authority. Provider accounting is recorded separately, without converting amounts or deriving a rate from fees. A new service-only accounting receipt can be recorded only after the original owned checkout, subscription, customer and quote are bound and the actual local payment is verified. Receipts are immutable per payment; a later paid cycle may append its own accounting receipt. Original USD subscriptions keep their strict USD contract.

Current-cycle accounting evidence uses the API's previous-payment timestamp to associate the snapshot with that payment. The next billing date can be edited and does not supply paid time. Both ends of the granted period come from the actual payment's immutable service period. A regression moves the provider next date and confirms that no additional paid time is granted.

An already anchored older payment can still be processed when the current API accounting snapshot has moved on. A proven current accounting tuple can also coexist with a late, exact older local payment: only the older payment's own period is applied, with no fabricated accounting receipt or newer-period access. If both the late payment's accounting evidence and the current accounting tuple are unproven, the event remains held for reconciliation. No monetary conversion is invented to clear that hold.

Stopping renewal and revoking a signed, verified refund do not grant access. These bounded paths retain exact owner, original checkout, subscription, customer, product, quote and monthly-term checks while allowing the customer to stop or revoke despite a changed accounting snapshot. A negative lifecycle event may record only a matching negative current provider state, with no payment grant, accounting receipt or grace extension. Active payments, renewals and unproven overdue grace cannot use that exception.

Final bounded implementation checks passed **160 tests across five files**, application TypeScript and four Live/Test billing/webhook Deno checks. Cases include actual handler composition, actual controller-to-PGlite SQL, separate local/accounting/settlement amounts, per-cycle revaluation, wrong-currency rejection, original-period recovery, cancellation, old-payment refund and negative-state boundaries. These counts overlap earlier runs. The additive accounting migration was independently reviewed; it does not modify grant logic or the already-applied localized-price migration. Native concurrency evidence and actual Test provider receipt replay are maintained by the coordinator and SQL reviewer.

No provider write, charge, purchase, credential inspection or deployment was performed by this agent. The coordinator must verify actual Test webhook replay and a distinct renewed local payment before claiming the provider journey works. Short operator-accelerated tests do not prove natural multi-month operation.
