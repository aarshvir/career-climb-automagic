Jobvance: 100-visitor PMF simulation

17 October 2026 · live jobvance.io plus the pricing page built but not yet published · invented personas

Verdict: no product-market fit yet.
  • None of the 100 simulated visitors would be "very disappointed" to lose Jobvance. The PMF bar is 40%.
  • Net Promoter Score is −88, and 11 of 100 would pay.
  • They loved how honest and fast it is. What lost them is that the result is too thin to beat free ChatGPT.
  • The homepage also leads with Job Loop, which most visitors can't try.
Trust is real, but it isn't yet a reason to come back. The fix is to deliver more truthful value per CV, not more features around it.

How this was run, and how far to trust it

  • Ground truth.
    • I used the live CV check myself with an invented 4-year marketing CV and a Senior Digital Marketing job ad. Results: 14 seconds to tailor, 3 changes (2 one-word swaps and a reorder), no profile summary, and 5 good gap questions with nowhere to answer them. The PDF preview took 4 seconds and the download needed one tick.
    • On mobile there is no overflow, but the "Tailor" button sits about 1.8 screens down.
    • Job Loop couldn't be tried without creating an account.
  • Visitors. 100 invented personas:
    • 45 UAE, 25 India, 11 USA, 8 UK, 11 other.
    • 53 on mobile, 47 on desktop.
    • 7 segments, each with its own urgency, tools, budget, privacy needs, traffic source and CV state.
  • The simulation. 5 independent Claude Sonnet agents, 20 personas each. Every agent read the same page snapshots and walkthrough and was told to respect real base rates: most visitors leave, a minority try, and very few pay on a first visit.
  • Caveat. Simulated users are not customers. They tend to be harsher than real people when told to be realistic, so treat the absolute numbers as directional. The pattern is reliable because my own hands-on test shows the same core problem: a thin result. Real PMF needs the survey described at the end, run with real users.

The most valuable segments, senior and mid-career, are also the most demanding. They pay for repositioning and discretion, not for word swaps.

What they loved: your real moat

  • Every change shown beside the original, with "Keep my original". This was the most-cited "aha".
  • The honesty guard. "It left out an AI suggestion that added something not in my CV", and it rejects "led" where you wrote "supported". Several said Gemini and ChatGPT quietly invent tools and claims.
  • Speed and polish: about 14 seconds to tailor, an exact PDF preview in 4 seconds, a one-tick download, and the job title read from the ad.
  • Privacy options: "Compare without AI" runs on the device.
  • One-time passes, nothing renews, with a 7-day money-back promise. These were praised wherever pricing was seen.

Truthful AI tailoring is a defensible position. The product just doesn't yet turn it into a result people can feel.

Recommended next 30 days

  1. Ship "Deep tailor" and the requirement-coverage view (gaps 1–2), about 1 week of build. This is the single change most likely to move "very disappointed" from zero.
  2. Re-cut the homepage around the instant tool, with Job Loop as an honest beta that has a coverage list (gap 3). This needs your decision against Codex's direction.
  3. Mobile: a job-link field and a remembered CV (gap 4).
  4. Trust strip on /check: the named AI provider, "not stored", and a sample before/after (gap 5).
  5. Pricing: keep passes, delay the free-tier cut until steps 1–2 ship, and add a coach plan and regional prices (gap 6).
  6. Measure real PMF. After a user's second tailored CV, ask the Sean Ellis question in the app. You need at least 40 answers.

Targets: 40% "very disappointed" among users with 2 or more CVs, 40% returning within 7 days, and 3% or more of activated users buying a pass.
