# Jobvance customer-perspective audit methodology

Audit date: 11 October 2026, Asia/Dubai. Read-only product audit requested by the owner. No release, product changes, advertising, outreach, payments, applications, or mailbox connection were authorized or performed for this audit.

## Review independence

Six separately prompted AI agents review six customer contexts. There are three available reviewer slots, so they run in two parallel batches. Each receives the same scoring rubric and product address, but no other reviewer's findings or scores. They inspect their own live public journeys and source paths. The coordinator receives findings and synthesizes them only after individual assessments. These are simulated customer perspectives from the same model family, not six humans, user interviews, an external certification, or a statistically independent sample.

The inspected source is the released checkout at `2d1ad90356a7c952740dde2c9bf02ab5880a1414`. The release receipt records version 10. Integration state is drawn from that same-day release evidence where current authenticated inspection is unavailable. Provider funding, configured secrets, external Google approval and actual hiring outcomes are not independently established by this audit.

## Evidence labels

- **LIVE:** Directly observed current public browser UI or device-only fictional guest flow.
- **SOURCE:** A reachable route or implementation verified in the current checkout. Source proves intended mechanics, not successful live operation or customer satisfaction.
- **RELEASE:** Existing first-party hosted test evidence from the prior release, explicitly reused. It is not a fresh independent customer test.
- **INFERENCE:** The reviewer's reasoned hypothesis about friction, demand or willingness to pay.
- **NOT VERIFIED:** An unavailable measurement or journey. No claim of execution is made.

Confidence is high for directly observed UI/clear source facts, medium for an inferred customer consequence, and low for untested retention, conversion, hiring or commercial outcomes. Prior passing tests establish tested mechanics only.

## Common scorecard

Each reviewer assigns a judgment score from 0 to 100 to each dimension, explains its evidence, then computes the weighted total. These are product-readiness judgments, not measured satisfaction, hiring probability, AI-detector percentages, market rankings or validated psychometric scores. Decimal totals show arithmetic only; a one-point difference should not be interpreted as meaningful.

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Audience need / positioning | 10% |
| Onboarding / time to first value | 15% |
| CV-to-vacancy evidence / output quality | 15% |
| UI / interaction design | 10% |
| Complete Job Loop delivery | 15% |
| Live vacancy discovery | 10% |
| Application tracking | 5% |
| Privacy / trust / user control | 10% |
| Mobile / accessibility | 5% |
| Repeat use / commercial readiness | 5% |

Anchors: 0 = missing/broken; 50 = usable with major friction; 75 = strong with material gaps; 90+ = exceptional with supporting evidence. Unknown performance or business outcomes are labeled unavailable rather than fabricated. A disabled capability can be scored low for current task readiness while still being distinguished from a defect.

The consolidated six-perspective number is the unweighted mean of the six weighted totals. It is a breadth-of-review indicator, not an estimate weighted by actual customer counts. A separate average of students, MBA students and employed career switchers represents the owner's three stated audiences. No actual audience distribution is available.

## Best-in-class boundary

Public feature pages from established alternatives are used as benchmarks for journey expectations; their advertised metrics are not independently validated, and their authenticated products are not scored or ranked without testing. No score of 98 or 100, employment guarantee, or claim that a development team worked for twelve months is imposed.

Customer validation and activation analytics are unavailable. We therefore do not calculate a measured activation score, invent a biggest funnel-drop step, or claim proven time savings. Proposed completion and retention thresholds in the improvement plan are future acceptance targets.
