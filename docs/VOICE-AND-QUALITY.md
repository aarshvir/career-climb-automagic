# Jobvance voice and product finish

Founder preference, 11 October 2026: the product should feel natural, carefully written and professionally finished. The requested “20–30% AI” figure is a style ambition, not a verified percentage of authorship or an enforceable detector result.

## Writing standard

- Write to one person preparing an application: direct, practical and respectful.
- Give the next action first. Use short instructions, then explain the detail where it matters.
- Prefer “your CV says you supported the work” to “related wording is not promoted into a claim.”
- Keep the original CV and vacancy quotations exact. Do not rewrite evidence to make it sound smoother.
- Avoid slogans, repeated technical caveats, unsupported hiring claims and invented customer stories.
- Keep important limits visible: a wording match is not a qualification decision; private saving needs consent; the user approves the final CV.
- Let the user sound like themselves. A clear verb, their own contribution and accurate dates/numbers matter more than swapping words to satisfy a detector. Do not silently replace responsibilities, achievements, qualifications or ownership claims.
- Never add a badge claiming a CV is “70% human,” “under 30% AI,” or written by a named team without reliable evidence. Do not claim that Jobvance took 30 developers or 12 months to build.

## Customer experience standard

The next action should be obvious on a phone. Show the current step, keep progress visible when moving forward, explain errors in plain language, distinguish loading from an empty account, and provide a way back without losing the current check. Use accurate success messages and retain approval, account isolation and privacy protections.

## Changes in this pass

Revised the homepage description, feature/how-to/FAQ copy, six-step instructions, evidence explanations, review questions and pilot positioning line. Normalized comparison terms are available in a disclosure instead of cluttering the main finding. A brief read-aloud review helps the user check their CV voice without changing their text. Google tab-storage consent keeps its scope, timing and shared-tab warning beside the shorter checkbox label. Step navigation now keeps the progress indicator in view.

The evidence rules, statuses, quotation spans, factual CV contents, private saving, approval gates, pricing availability and billing flags were not changed. No external AI rewrite or detector API was added; no customer CV was sent to a detector.

## Review method and its limits

Review the copy against the audience and tasks above. A local humanizer script can flag word choice, sentence rhythm and passive wording. Its heuristic “humanity score” is only an editing prompt, not an AI-authorship test or a conversion metric. Short interface instructions can score poorly on sentence variance even when they are useful; do not lengthen them merely to raise a score.

Research has documented false positives and language bias in text detectors. [Stanford's research summary](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-detectors-biased-against-non-native-english-writers) describes a 2023 study; it is not a current benchmark of every detector. [OpenAI's classifier notice](https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/) records the withdrawal of that classifier for low accuracy. Neither supports a guarantee that every future checker will return a particular percentage.
